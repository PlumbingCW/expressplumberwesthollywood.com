Sewer Scope Before You Buy or Remodel in West Hollywood: What You Get

A camera on a flexible rod can travel through a sewer lateral and show you everything the pipe has been hiding. Buyers use it to avoid a surprise bill, owners use it after repeat clogs and managers use it to settle questions of cause.

This guide explains what you should receive, how to read the findings and when the scope should be done.

What a good scope report includes

You should receive the video, a summary of findings with the distance and depth of each, and a note of the pipe material. A clear report separates serious defects from minor ones, and it tells you what the plumber recommends.

Ask whether the line was cleaned first. A camera cannot see through a full blockage, and a line that was jetted before the scope will look better than it behaves day to day.

When to scope

Scope before closing on a purchase, before a remodel that adds fixtures, after repeat clogs and after a nearby tree has been removed or has grown. In a multi-unit building, a scope after a backup shows whether the problem is in one unit, in a shared stack or in the lateral.

The detail is in our sewer inspection page. If the scope shows damage, sewer line repair explains the options.

Questions to ask before you book

A good scope answers all of these.

  • Do I get the video?You need it for a seller, a manager or an insurer.
  • Is the line located and marked?Location and depth matter for any repair.
  • Is the line cleaned first if needed?A blocked line cannot be inspected.
  • Who is responsible for each finding?Private lateral and city main are different.

A sample of what the camera finds, in plain terms

A typical lateral in an older building shows a few things at once. The pipe near the building may be cast iron with rough, scaled walls. Further out, it may change to clay, with joints every few feet. At one of those joints, roots may have entered and spread into a fan.

Each of those is a piece of information. Scale tells you the bore has narrowed, and roots tell you a joint is open. Offset joints tell you the ground has moved. A good report puts each one on a map with a distance from the cleanout, so a repair crew knows where to start.

Why the video matters in a sale

Sewer laterals are not part of a standard home inspection, and a seller may not know what is inside the line. A scope before closing gives the buyer facts instead of guesses, and it lets both sides negotiate with the same information.

If the scope shows a collapse or a major defect, the buyer can ask for a repair, a credit or a lower price. If it shows a clean line, the buyer has one less worry. In both cases, the cost of the scope is small next to the cost of an unexpected replacement.

What a scope does not tell you

Know the limits.

  • The condition of the city mainThe camera usually stops at the connection.
  • The futureRoots grow and ground moves.
  • Parts of the line it cannot reachSharp bends and full blockages stop the camera.
  • The cause of every backupSome backups come from the main or a shared stack.

Using the findings with an owner or manager

A manager can use the video to decide between cleaning and repair, and to explain the choice to the owner. A tenant can use it to show that a backup came from the line and not from misuse.

Ask for a copy of the footage, the written summary and the location notes. File them with the property papers, and share them with whoever holds the repair budget.

Choosing who does the scope

A scope is only as good as the person running it. Ask whether the plumber locates and marks the line, writes a summary and keeps the footage. A short clip with no notes is not a report.

Ask who will explain the findings. A plumber who can point to a crack on the screen and say what it means is worth more than a stack of files. The best reports also say what is fine, since a clean section is useful information.

Finally, ask what the plumber would do next. A scope that ends with a repair plan, a cost range and a schedule helps you decide. A scope that ends with a sales pitch for a full replacement, with no footage to back it up, deserves a second opinion.

Timing a scope around a purchase

Order the scope during the inspection period, not after it. A line that needs cleaning first should be cleaned in time for the camera to pass, and a scope that finds a defect needs time for quotes and negotiation. A few days of planning avoids a rushed decision.

Who owns what in West Hollywood

The City of West Hollywood says its crews clean city-owned sewer mainlines and respond around the clock, and it advises owners to have their private lateral video inspected and cleaned routinely as needed. Sewage flows to the Hyperion plant in Playa del Rey. The lateral from your building to the main is the owner's, including the connection.

Work entirely on private property can be done by a plumber with a C-36 license. Work in the public right-of-way needs an encroachment permit, an A or C-34 license and proof of insurance. If you are buying in Beverly Hills or the hills, the rules differ, and our Beverly Hills and Hollywood Hills pages explain them.
