When jetting becomes an emergency job

Jetting is often scheduled, but backups bring it forward. A restaurant line that has filled with grease, a main line full of roots, or a building that backs up every time a few units run water at once are common emergency triggers. The longer sewage sits in a failing line, the more damage it does to floors and finishes.

In busy commercial strips like Santa Monica Boulevard and the Sunset Strip, kitchen lines carry heavy grease loads. A line that is jetted on a schedule rarely turns into a midnight call, which is why many food businesses book recurring service.

How hydro jetting works

A hose with a jetting nozzle is fed into the line through a cleanout. Water at high pressure exits the nozzle in several directions, pushing the nozzle forward and scouring the wall as it returns. The debris flushes toward the sewer, and the pipe is cleaner than any cable can leave it.

We pick pressure and nozzle for the pipe. Cast iron, clay, ABS and PVC tolerate different pressures, and a pipe that is already broken can be damaged by an aggressive jet. That is why a camera inspection is part of the plan whenever the pipe condition is unknown.

Roots, grease and scale: what jetting removes best

Grease is the most common cause of repeat kitchen and building-line clogs, and jetting is the most reliable way to remove it. Roots enter a line at joints and cracks. Jetting cuts and flushes hair roots and clears the line, but it does not seal the entry point, so roots tend to return if the joint is not repaired.

Scale and mineral deposits appear in older cast iron, which is common in buildings that predate modern pipe materials. A jet can scour the rough interior surface, and a camera shows whether the pipe underneath is sound or thin.

Where the work happens: private line versus city main

The City of West Hollywood cleans its own sewer mainlines, and property owners maintain the private laterals that connect to them. Work that stays inside the property line can be done by a plumber with a C-36 license. Work in the public right-of-way needs a city encroachment permit and a contractor with an A or C-34 license.

Jetting from a cleanout inside the property is private-side work. If our camera shows the blockage sits in the city main, we tell you and the city handles that part.
