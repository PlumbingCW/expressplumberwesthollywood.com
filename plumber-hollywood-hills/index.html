High pressure on a slope

Water pressure at the bottom of a hill can run high, and the California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator when static pressure is above 80 psi. A regulator that is worn or missing puts every pipe, valve and heater under strain. Many hillside leaks that look random trace back to pressure.

We test pressure at a hose bib and replace a failing regulator. This one check often prevents the next leak, and it costs less than a repiping job. Pressure and the connected leak question are covered under leak detection.

Why hillside leaks hide

Many Hollywood Hills houses sit on stepped foundations, with supply lines running under floors, through garages and along retaining walls. A leak may drain downhill and show up as a damp patch or a stain far from the source. A meter that moves with every fixture closed tells you there is a leak even when you cannot see it.

We isolate the line by section, listen with acoustic gear and check with thermal imaging. If the house sits on a slab, the question becomes slab leak repair, and we say plainly whether the leak is under concrete or somewhere easier to reach.

Sewer laterals and what the City of Los Angeles expects of owners

The Hollywood Hills sit inside the City of Los Angeles, not West Hollywood. LA Sanitation owns the sewer mainline, and the property owner owns the whole lateral from the house to the main. A backwater valve needs a permit from the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety.

LA Sanitation says that over half of sewer backups come from roots and fats, oils and grease. The city also offers a lateral repair rebate for eligible rate-paying owners, applied for through MyLA311, and we suggest checking the current terms before work begins. We provide the camera footage and written findings that an application may ask for.
