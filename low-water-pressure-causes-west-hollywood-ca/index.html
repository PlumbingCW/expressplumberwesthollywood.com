Low Water Pressure in West Hollywood: Causes, Quick Checks and When to Call

Low pressure feels like one problem and has many causes. One weak shower head is a scale problem. Weak flow at every tap in the unit is a supply problem. A change after street work or a break may come from the utility.

This guide sorts the causes by where the symptom shows up, then tells you how to measure pressure and what the code says about a safe range.

Where the symptom shows up and what it means

Start with the pattern.

PatternLikely causeFirst check
One fixture onlyClogged aerator or cartridgeClean or replace the part
Hot water onlyHeater sediment or a partly closed valveCheck the heater valves
Cold water onlyFailing valve or a supply restrictionCheck the cold valve
Whole unitRegulator, main valve or service lineMeasure pressure
Whole building after street workUtility sideCall the utility
Upper floors onlyUtility pressure or a supply problemAsk the manager and the utility

Measure before you guess

A hose bib pressure gauge costs little and screws onto an outdoor tap. Close every other fixture, open the tap and read the number. The California Plumbing Code calls for a regulator when static pressure exceeds 80 psi, set at 80 psi or less. A very low reading, below about 40 psi, is a sign of a restriction or a utility issue.

Take the reading at different times of day. Pressure can dip at peak demand. If readings are consistently low, the plumber tests the regulator and checks for a restriction.

Clogs, scale and old galvanized pipe

Aerators and shower heads collect scale. Unscrew them, soak them in vinegar and rinse. If pressure returns, that was the cause. If every fixture is weak, the supply pipe itself may be narrowed. Galvanized steel rusts from inside and the bore closes slowly.

In an older building, a repipe of the affected zone may be the fix. See repiping for how that is scoped. A hidden leak can also lower pressure, and the leak detection page explains how we find one.

Quick checks before you call

These take minutes.

  • Check the main valveA partly closed valve restricts the whole unit.
  • Clean aerators and shower headsScale is the most common cause.
  • Check the regulatorA failing regulator drops pressure.
  • Ask neighborsIf they have the same issue, it may be the utility.

When the utility is the cause

If pressure fell across the building after street work or a main break, the problem may be on the utility side. LADWP reported that some customers on higher floors saw low pressure during the July 2026 Sunset Boulevard event. Call the utility customer service line and report it.

If the utility says its side is fine, then your service line or building plumbing is the next place to look, and a pressure test at the meter side helps. See water line repair.

The partly closed valve nobody remembers

Valves get bumped during repairs and meter work. A main shutoff that is only three-quarters open restricts flow to the whole unit, and the symptom looks like a failing line. Gate valves with a wheel handle are the usual culprits, since they can sit partly open without any sign.

Open the valve fully and test again. If you have a ball valve with a lever, it should sit in line with the pipe when open. Replacing an old gate valve with a ball valve is a low-cost upgrade and it stops the problem from returning.

Why hot water pressure can drop alone

If only the hot side is weak, the water heater is the first suspect. Sediment in the tank, a partly closed heater valve or a scaled-up dip tube can restrict flow. A tankless unit with scale shows the same sign, as covered in our tankless filter guide.

Flushing the tank and checking the valves often restores flow. If it does not, the heater or the hot line needs a closer look.

When low pressure is a safety issue

Some cases are not just annoying.

  • Fire sprinkler or sprinkler-connected supply in a buildingReport it to the manager and the utility.
  • A sudden drop with wet ground near the meterA service line may be leaking.
  • Brown water with low pressureSediment or corroded pipe.
  • Low pressure and a running meterA leak is likely.

How a plumber tests pressure and flow

A plumber takes a static pressure reading with the gauge on a hose bib and then a flow reading with a tap open. Static pressure shows what the system offers at rest. Flow shows what you get when water is moving, and a big drop between the two points to a restriction.

Readings at the meter side and at the farthest fixture show where the loss happens. A drop near the main points to the valve or the service line. A drop at one branch points to scale or a kink in that run.

Those readings turn a vague complaint into a short list of causes, and they keep you from replacing parts that were never the problem.

West Hollywood utilities and your pressure

LADWP serves most of West Hollywood, and Beverly Hills Water serves certain west side areas, so a pressure complaint goes to the utility named on your bill. Hilly areas of the city can see very different pressure from street level to the top of a slope. In the hills, a regulator is more important than in the flats.

Hard water also affects flow. Beverly Hills Water reports about 290 mg/L, or roughly 17 grains per gallon, and LADWP values vary by source. Scale reduces flow in fixtures and heaters over time. Our Hollywood Hills page and Beverly Hills page describe the local picture, and the softener and filtration page explains treatment.
