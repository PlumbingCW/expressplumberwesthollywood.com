Plumber in the Eastside, West Hollywood, CA

The Eastside covers the eastern end of West Hollywood, around La Brea Avenue and the Santa Monica Boulevard corridor. Small and medium residential buildings sit north and south of the boulevard, with shops and restaurants along it.

Call (213) 672-5590 if you live or run a business in the area. Describe the problem and the address, and ask about the earliest appointment available.

The Eastside on the map

The West Hollywood Chamber of Commerce describes the Eastside as bounded by North La Brea Avenue to the east, Fountain Avenue to the north, North Hayworth Avenue to the west and the city's southern boundary. It says small and medium-scale residential areas lie north and south of Santa Monica Boulevard and that the Gateway development at La Brea Avenue and Santa Monica Boulevard added hundreds of residential units.

The city adopted the Eastside Community Plan on October 16, 2017, covering the former Eastside Redevelopment area, with housing as one of its key topics. The neighborhood watch list adds streets such as Formosa, Detroit, Spaulding, Curson, Lexington, Norton and Hampton, and the Plummer Park watch group covers the 1100 and 1200 blocks of Fuller, Vista and Gardner. Sources also describe a long-established Russian-speaking community and locally oriented markets.

Older apartments next to new buildings

The Preservation Alliance lists the Plummer Park Apartment Grouping, five two-story brick apartment buildings from 1929 and 1933 on North Vista Street along the west edge of Plummer Park, designated by the city in 1992. A few blocks away, the Gateway project is recent construction. A resident can live between a 1930s building and a new one on the same walk.

That mix means two different conversations. In an older small building, ask management whether a recurring clog is in a shared line. In newer construction, ask whether a repair is covered by a building warranty or an HOA before you pay for it.

Shops, restaurants and commercial plumbing along Santa Monica Boulevard

The boulevard has a mix of regional and local retail, according to the Chamber. Businesses have needs homes do not.

  • Grease and floor drains. Kitchens that serve food need regular drain care. See hydro jetting for buildup and drain cleaning for routine clogs.
  • Restrooms. High-use toilets and sinks fail more often than ones in a house. See commercial plumbing.
  • After-hours problems. A leak in a closed shop is still a leak. Call and tell the line the building is unattended.

Eastside plumbing questions answered

The Chamber of Commerce gives La Brea Avenue to the east, Fountain Avenue to the north, Hayworth Avenue to the west and the city's southern boundary. Other sources describe it somewhat differently.

Sources describe small and medium-scale residential areas on both sides of Santa Monica Boulevard plus newer mixed-use buildings. No source in this research gave a precise housing split for the Eastside alone.

You can call (213) 672-5590 and describe the business and the problem. The person who answers will tell you what is available.

The Eastside is inside the City of West Hollywood, so its plumbing code and permit rules apply. Areas just east of La Brea Avenue belong to the City of Los Angeles, which has different rules.
