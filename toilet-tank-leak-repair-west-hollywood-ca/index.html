Toilet Tank Leaking in West Hollywood? Find the Cause and Fix It

A leaking toilet tank can mean a slow trickle into the bowl, a wet floor under the tank or a tank that sweats on a warm day. Each has a different cause. Close the supply valve behind the toilet before you start, and keep towels nearby.

This guide walks through the checks in the order a plumber would do them, and tells you where a do-it-yourself fix stops making sense. If water is spreading, skip to the end and call.

Start with the dye test

Remove the tank lid, drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank and do not flush. Wait fifteen minutes. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and water is seeping past it all day. That is the most common tank problem and the least costly to fix.

If the bowl stays clear but the floor is wet, the leak is outside the tank-to-bowl path. Dry the tank and the floor, then look at the supply line, the tank bolts and the tank body.

Flappers, fill valves and the overflow tube

The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank. It warps with age and mineral buildup, and then it lets water slip into the bowl. A replacement costs little and takes a few minutes. Match the size to your flush valve, which is usually two or three inches.

The fill valve shuts off water when the tank reaches its level. If the water level is above the top of the overflow tube, water spills down the tube and into the bowl without a flush. Lower the float or replace the valve. A running toilet can waste a surprising amount of water without a sound.

Tank bolts, gaskets and the tank-to-bowl seal

Two or three bolts hold the tank to the bowl, each with a rubber washer. Those washers harden over time and the bolts corrode. A drip that appears only after a flush often comes from here. Tighten gently, a little at a time. Porcelain cracks if you overtighten.

If the bolts are rusted, replace them with brass or stainless hardware and fresh washers. This job needs the tank drained and removed from the bowl in many cases, and it is a point where a plumber saves you from a cracked tank.

Condensation: the leak that is not a leak

A tank that drips on all sides and feels cold is sweating. Cold water in the tank meets warm humid air and moisture forms on the porcelain. Los Angeles is dry for most of the year, so sweating shows up in bathrooms with hot showers and poor venting.

Run the exhaust fan, crack a window during showers and wipe the tank. A tank liner or an anti-sweat valve helps in stubborn cases. If water pools under the toilet even when the bathroom is dry, it is a true leak.

When to stop and call a plumber

These signs mean the repair is beyond a quick part swap.

  • Water at the base after every flushThe wax ring or flange has failed and the subfloor may be at risk.
  • A cracked tank or bowlPorcelain cannot be patched safely.
  • A shutoff valve that will not closeForcing it can break the valve and flood the room.
  • A leak that appears in the ceiling belowIn a multi-floor building, tell the manager at once.

Why a toilet tank leak is easy to underestimate

Tank leaks rarely announce themselves. A flapper that leaks into the bowl makes no mess, so nobody notices. The water meter does, and the bill climbs month after month before anyone looks.

That quiet loss is the reason the dye test comes first. It takes a few minutes, costs nothing and answers the question before you buy parts you do not need. If you do find a leak, replace the flapper, the chain and the fill valve together, since they wear at the same pace.

What to buy and what to skip

A short parts list covers most tank repairs.

  • A flapper that matches your flush valveTwo-inch and three-inch sizes are common. Take the old one to the store.
  • A universal fill valveIt replaces most older fill valves without changing the tank.
  • Brass or stainless tank bolts with rubber washersLow-grade steel bolts rust and leak again within a year.
  • A braided stainless supply lineReplace it whenever you disturb the connection.
  • Skip tank cleaning tabletsBleach and strong cleaners can rot rubber parts and shorten flapper life.

What West Hollywood buildings add to a tank leak

West Hollywood is about 80 percent renters, since the census reports an owner-occupied rate of 20.5 percent for 2019 to 2023. If you rent, report a leaking tank to your manager in writing, because water that reaches a floor can travel to the unit below.

Many buildings here have an original certificate of occupancy before July 1, 1979, which puts them under rent stabilization. Older fixtures and shutoff valves are common in those buildings. A stiff valve is a reason to ask the owner for a replacement before an emergency, and a toilet repair visit can include that. Renters in Hollywood and owners in Beverly Hills see the same wear, and a worn tank often goes hand in hand with older fixtures and shutoffs.
