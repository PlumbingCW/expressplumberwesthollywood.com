What a call from Hollywood sounds like

Most Hollywood calls come from apartment buildings and small businesses. A tenant on the third floor reports water in the ceiling of the second, a restaurant reports a floor drain backing up on a busy night or a manager reports no hot water for the whole building. The dispatcher asks which units are affected, where the shutoff is and who can give access.

That information decides the truck. A shared stack needs a camera and cabling gear. A building water heater needs parts or a replacement unit. A leak between floors needs tools to find it before any wall is opened.

A cable opens the line, and a jet scours the wall. A schedule keeps the next midnight call from happening.

Sewer lines in the City of Los Angeles

Hollywood is part of the City of Los Angeles. LA Sanitation owns the sewer mainline, and the property owner owns the whole lateral from the building to the main. A backwater valve needs a permit from the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety.

The city notes that more than half of sewer backups come from roots and fats, oils and grease, and that a lateral repair rebate exists for eligible rate-paying owners through MyLA311. Our camera footage and written findings support that kind of request, and sewer line repair covers the repair options.
