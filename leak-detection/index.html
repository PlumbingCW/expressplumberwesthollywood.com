Emergency Leak Detection in West Hollywood, CA

A hidden leak is urgent when water is spreading or the meter will not stop. We take leak detection calls in West Hollywood, CA 24/7. While you wait, shut the main valve for the unit, switch off power to wet areas only if it is safe, and move belongings away from damp walls.

Hidden leak signs that are urgent

Watch for a meter that moves while every fixture is off, warm spots on a floor, peeling paint, bubbling plaster, a hissing sound in a wall and new stains on a ceiling below your unit. Each one points to water escaping the system somewhere you cannot see.

In a multi-story West Hollywood building, a leak in one unit often shows up in a neighbor's ceiling first. Tell your property manager early, because the source may be a shared riser rather than your own supply line.

How we find the leak

The method moves from least to most invasive.

  1. 1
    Meter testWe close every fixture and watch the meter. If the dial moves, water is escaping somewhere on the supply side.
  2. 2
    IsolateWe shut valves in stages to learn which branch or floor holds the leak.
  3. 3
    Acoustic listeningGround microphones and contact sensors pick up the sound of water escaping under pressure.
  4. 4
    Thermal imagingA thermal camera shows temperature differences from hot or cold water behind a surface.
  5. 5
    Confirm and markWe mark the spot so an opening in the wall or floor is small and in the right place.

The high water bill question

A bill that jumps with no change in habits is often a toilet that runs, an irrigation line that stays open or a slow underground leak. The meter test settles whether the water is leaving the plumbing or being used.

LADWP serves most of West Hollywood, and Beverly Hills Water serves certain west side areas. Your bill tells you which utility you have, and the meter belongs to that utility. We never touch the utility side of the meter, and we point you to the right customer service line if the meter itself looks faulty.

What older buildings add to the picture

Many West Hollywood buildings are covered by rent stabilization because their original certificate of occupancy predates July 1, 1979. Plumbing from that era can include galvanized steel and early copper, both of which develop pinhole leaks with age. A single leak in such a system often means other sections are close behind.

We tell you whether the leak looks like an isolated failure or a sign of general pipe wear. If it is the second, the conversation moves toward repiping rather than another patch.

What to do while you wait

These steps limit damage.

  • Find and close the shutoffYour unit or building has a main valve. Learn where it is before an emergency.
  • Move belongingsLift items off wet floors and away from walls.
  • Photograph the damageInsurers ask for photos taken before cleanup.
  • Do not open walls yourselfA small hole in the wrong place adds repair cost without finding the leak.
