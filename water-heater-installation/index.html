Water Heater Installation in West Hollywood, CA

Installing a water heater where none existed, moving one, or converting from gas to electric is a different job from swapping a tank that has failed. This page is for planned work in West Hollywood, CA: remodels, new closets, unit conversions and upgrades done on your schedule.

If your tank is leaking right now, start with emergency water heater replacement instead. For everything else, a good installation starts with the location, the fuel and the venting, long before any pipe is cut.

Start with where the heater will live

Location decides the rest of the job. A heater in a garage can use a standard vent and a floor drain. A heater in an interior closet needs combustion air and a vent path to the outdoors if it is gas, and a drain pan with a drain line if water damage would reach a finished floor.

In a West Hollywood condo or small apartment building, the closet is often the only option. Check the clearances on the new unit before you buy it, because a 50-gallon tank and a 40-gallon tank can have very different heights once the vent and relief line are added.

Rules and approvals in West Hollywood

The West Hollywood Plumbing Code requires a permit for installation, alteration or replacement of regulated plumbing systems, and each building or structure needs its own permit. Because rules vary by job, confirm with the Building and Safety Division before you buy equipment.

State law also requires water heaters up to 120 gallons to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement. This applies to new and replacement installations, and it is one of the first things an inspector or a home buyer checks.

If you rent, tenants cannot normally change the heater on their own. Landlord approval is part of the plan, and in a building covered by the city rent stabilization ordinance, a capital improvement is handled by the owner.

Water quality and the equipment you choose

West Hollywood water comes mostly from the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power, with Beverly Hills Water serving certain western areas. Beverly Hills publishes a hardness of about 290 mg/L, or 17 grains per gallon, on its water page. LADWP lists hardness by source in its water quality tables, with different sources reading in different ranges.

Harder water leaves more scale on heating elements and burner surfaces. That does not change which heater you can install, but it does change how often you should flush it and whether a softener or filter is worth considering for a tankless unit.

Existing condition

Old shutoff valves and corroded pipes are replaced while the lines are open.

A planned installation is a home improvement, not an insurable loss, so insurance rarely helps. A properly permitted job protects you later if a buyer or insurer asks for records.

Choosing between a tank and a tankless unit

If you are replacing a failed unit, read about water heater replacement first. If you want on-demand hot water, compare it with a tankless water heater. Scale control matters for either, and softener and filtration is the long-term answer.
