Plastic Pipes in West Hollywood Buildings: PVC, ABS, CPVC and PEX Compared

Plastic pipe is everywhere in modern plumbing, and the type matters. A pipe fine for a drain can fail on a hot supply line. Knowing what you have helps you buy parts and talk to a plumber.

This guide compares the four common types, shows how to tell them apart and explains what the older ones tell you about the age of your building.

The four plastics and where they belong

Each has a role.

PipeUseColor or signLimit
PVCDrain, waste, vent and cold supply where permittedWhite, rigidNot for hot water
ABSDrain, waste and ventBlack, rigidNot for pressure supply
CPVCHot and cold supplyCream or light yellow, rigidBrittle with age or high heat
PEXHot and cold supplyRed, blue or white, flexibleNot for sunlight or flue contact

Drain pipes: PVC and ABS

Both carry waste by gravity. ABS is black and common in many parts of California, and PVC is white. They use different cement, and joining one to the other needs a transition fitting and the right cement. Mixing materials with the wrong cement is a cause of drain leaks.

A drain that is part cast iron and part plastic is common in renovated buildings. The transition uses a rubber coupling with stainless bands. If a coupling is loose, it leaks at the join.

Supply pipes: CPVC and PEX

CPVC is rigid and takes solvent cement. It handles hot water but becomes brittle as it ages, so a CPVC system in an older building can crack at fittings. PEX is flexible and tolerates movement. We cover the details in the post on installing PEX plumbing.

Neither is a good choice for gas, and neither belongs in direct sunlight. Both need proper supports and clearances.

How to tell what you have

Check these places.

  • Look at the colorWhite, black, cream and red or blue point to PVC, ABS, CPVC and PEX.
  • Read the printingPipe is stamped with its type and rating.
  • Check under the sinkVisible drain and supply pipes tell the story.
  • Ask your owner or managerBuilding records may list the material.

Why old pipes matter more than new plastic

Plastic pipes rarely cause trouble early. Problems come from the older materials around them. A building with a plastic remodel in one unit may still have cast iron stacks and galvanized supply in the walls. The joins between old and new are where leaks begin.

If you see a mix of materials, ask a plumber to look at the transitions. A camera inspection shows what is inside a drain you cannot see.

Plastic pipes and fire, heat and sun

Plastic softens with heat. CPVC tolerates hot water but not a flame, PEX should stay away from a flue and PVC should never carry hot water. Water heater connections normally use a short section of copper or a rated flexible connector between the tank and plastic pipe.

Sunlight damages PEX and weakens other plastics over time. Pipe that runs along an exposed garage wall or past a window needs a cover or a different material. If you see chalky, faded plastic outside, plan to replace it.

Which plastic for which job

A quick reference for owners.

JobCommon choiceWhy
Toilet and sink drainABS or PVCRigid and low cost, glued joints
Hot and cold supply in wallsPEX or CPVCRated for pressure and heat
Under-slab supplyPEX or copperNeeds protection and a sleeve
Vent through the roofABS or PVCNo pressure, light weight

What to do if you find an unknown pipe

Read the printing on the pipe, which usually lists the material, the size and the standard it meets. Take a photo and bring it to a plumber. Mixed systems from different decades are normal, and the mix itself is not a problem if each joint is made correctly.

The thing to look for is a transition between materials. Those joints are where leaks start, and where an old fitting meets a new pipe. A plumber can check each one and tell you which are fine and which deserve attention.

Buying and joining plastic pipe correctly

Each plastic has its own cement or fitting. PVC cement, ABS cement and CPVC cement are not the same, and the wrong one can leave a joint that looks sound and leaks under pressure. Primer matters on PVC and CPVC, and the pipe must be cut square and cleaned before joining.

Look for the markings on every length, including the standard it meets, the size and the intended use. Pipe stored in the sun can be brittle before it is installed, so buy fresh stock and store it in the shade.

If a project mixes materials, ask for the right transition fittings, not a hardware store workaround. A transition that is rated for both materials lasts for decades. One that is not often leaks in months.

A note on warranties and listings

Plastic pipe and fittings carry listings that show which standard they meet and where they may be used. A fitting without a listing, or a pipe used outside its listing, can void a warranty and fail inspection. Keep the packaging or take a photo of the markings before you cover a run.

What this means for West Hollywood buildings

West Hollywood rent stabilization covers buildings with an original certificate of occupancy before July 1, 1979. In those buildings the supply may be galvanized or copper, the drains may be cast iron or clay, and plastic is often a later repair. Plastic in an old building is a clue that a section was replaced, and the age of that repair tells you what may be next.

The California Plumbing Code governs which plastics are allowed where, and local code adds permit rules. Ask the city before replacing piping. Our repiping page explains whole-building replacement, and the Hollywood page and Hollywood Hills page cover the neighborhoods we visit.
