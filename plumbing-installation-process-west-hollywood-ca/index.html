How Plumbing Installation Works in a West Hollywood Remodel

A remodel schedule lives or dies on plumbing sequence. The pipes go in before drywall, and the fixtures go on after paint. If you plan the order well, nothing is opened twice.

This guide describes the phases of a plumbing installation in a remodel, what decisions you need to make early and where West Hollywood rules and older buildings add steps.

Phase one: planning and fixture choices

Pick your fixtures before the plumber roughs in. A freestanding tub, a wall-hung toilet and a rain shower each need a different rough-in location and different backing in the wall. Changing the fixture after the walls close can mean opening them again.

Walk the space with your plumber and contractor. Mark each fixture location, the water heater, the shutoffs and any appliance connections. Decide whether you want a pressure regulator, a recirculation line or a softener before the walls close.

Rough-in: what goes in the wall

Rough-in is the pipe work you cannot see afterward. Supply lines run to each fixture, drains drop to the main, and vents let air in so drains flow. Slope matters on drains, and vent sizing matters for noise and trap protection.

This is the stage for choosing materials. PEX is fast to install and tolerates scale, and copper has a long record. We cover the choice in our blog post on installing PEX plumbing. Whichever you choose, ask for a pressure test before the walls close.

Permits and inspections

The West Hollywood Plumbing Code requires a permit for installation, alteration, repair, replacement or remodel of plumbing systems. Leak repairs and the clearing of stoppages in drain, soil, waste or vent piping are exempt, but replacing that piping needs a permit. Your plumber or contractor tells you who pulls it.

An inspection happens before walls are closed. A failed inspection is a delay, not a disaster, and it costs much less to fix open walls than closed ones. Keep the signed permit card for the property records.

Decisions to make before rough-in

Each one costs little now and a lot later.

  • Fixture list and locationsFinal models, not stand-ins.
  • Water heater planTank, tankless and location.
  • Shutoff valvesWhere each fixture can be isolated.
  • Access panelsWhere a future plumber can reach valves.
  • Filtration or softenerPlan the connection now if you want one.

Pressure, old pipes and surprises behind the wall

The California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator when static pressure is above 80 psi. Test before you design, because high pressure shortens the life of new fixtures. If old galvanized or cast iron appears when the wall opens, stop and review the plan. Repiping one zone while the wall is open often costs less than going back later.

Keeping the schedule honest

Plumbing sits in the middle of a remodel timeline. Demolition has to happen before rough-in, and rough-in has to pass inspection before drywall. A fixture delivered late holds up trim-out, and a permit delay holds up everything behind it.

Ask your plumber for a short written sequence with the days that need a dry building, the days that need water off and the inspections that gate the next trade. Share it with the contractor, the tile setter and the manager. Most remodel delays come from trades that did not know what the plumber needed.

Questions to ask before you sign

Good answers tell you how the job will run.

  • Who pulls the permit?The permit holder carries responsibility for the work passing inspection.
  • What is the water shutoff plan?In an occupied building, tenants need notice and a time window.
  • What happens if old pipe is found in the wall?You want the option and the price before the surprise, not after.
  • What does the warranty cover?Labor and parts are often covered differently.

The final test before you sign off

After trim-out, the plumber runs every fixture, checks hot and cold at each tap, flushes each toilet, fills each tub and checks the drains for speed and noise. You should see this done, not just hear that it was done.

Take photos of the shutoff valves and label them. Keep the permit card, the final inspection record and the warranty papers together. If a fixture leaks within the warranty, those papers decide who pays.

What older West Hollywood buildings add

West Hollywood rent stabilization covers properties with a certificate of occupancy before July 1, 1979, so many remodels happen in older buildings. Behind the walls you may find cast iron stacks, galvanized supply and drains that were never vented to current code. Each finding changes the plan, and good contractors tell you before the walls close.

The city lists the Southern California Gas Company as its gas utility and LADWP as the water utility for most addresses, with Beverly Hills Water serving some on the west side. Know which applies, because a service change or a meter question goes to the utility.
