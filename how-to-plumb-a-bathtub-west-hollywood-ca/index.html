How to Plumb a Bathtub in West Hollywood: Drain, Overflow, Valve and Vent

Plumbing a bathtub means connecting four things: a drain, an overflow, a supply valve with a spout and a vent. If any of them is wrong, the tub leaks, gurgles or drains slowly.

This guide describes each part in plain terms, so you can talk with your plumber and your contractor. Rough-in dimensions vary by tub and model, so use the manufacturer sheet.

Start with the tub and the sheet

Every tub has a spec sheet showing the drain position and the rough-in dimensions. A standard alcove tub sits against three walls and drains at one end. A freestanding tub may drain through the floor.

Set the tub in place before connecting anything, check that it is level and that it rests solidly on its base. A tub that flexes under load leaks at the drain.

The four parts of a tub installation

Each has a job and a common failure.

PartJobCommon mistake
Drain and trapCarries water away and holds a seal against sewer gasWrong slope or a missing trap
OverflowPrevents overfillingLoose gasket leaks into the wall
Valve and spoutMixes and delivers hot and cold waterReversed supplies or poor soldering
VentLets air in so water drains freelyNo vent or a vent too far away

Installation steps

The order below avoids opening a finished wall.

  1. 1
    Rough in the drain and trapSet the trap, the drain tailpiece and a slope of about a quarter inch per foot.
  2. 2
    Set the valveMount the valve at the right depth for the trim and test the supply lines.
  3. 3
    Install the tubSeat it on mortar or foam as the maker specifies.
  4. 4
    Connect drain and overflowUse the gaskets provided and test with water before closing access.
  5. 5
    Finish the wall and trimTile or surround goes on after the pressure test.

Access panels and why they matter

A tub drain often sits in a framed wall or under a floor. If the drain leaks after the wall is finished, the only way in is by cutting. A small access panel on the back side of the wall or in the floor below makes future repair possible.

In a multi-story building, that access may be through a closet in the unit below. Plan it with the owner and the neighbor before closing the walls. A hidden failure of a tub drain is a common source of ceiling stains and a classic leak detection call.

Mistakes that cause leaks later

Most are easy to prevent.

  • Skipping the water testFill the tub and check the drain before closing the wall.
  • Overtightening plastic partsIt cracks threads and leaks slowly.
  • Wrong thread sealantUse what the maker specifies on drains.
  • No access to the trapFuture repairs become demolition.

Valve choices and why they matter

A pressure-balancing valve holds the water temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet or runs a tap elsewhere. A thermostatic valve lets you set a precise temperature. A basic two-handle valve mixes by hand and costs the least.

Many codes call for a pressure-balancing or thermostatic valve in new showers and tubs, to reduce scald risk. Check what applies to your project before buying. The valve body sits inside the wall, so changing it later means opening the wall.

Questions to settle before the walls close

Each costs little to answer now.

  • Which side is the drain on?Left and right tubs are not interchangeable.
  • Where does the valve sit?Height and depth must suit the trim and the tile.
  • Is there blocking for grab bars?Solid wood in the wall costs little today.
  • Is the tub on a solid base?A flexing tub loosens the drain.

Water heater and hot water delay

A tub is a big hot water user. A heater that is too small leaves the last person with a lukewarm bath. Check the heater size when you plan a soaking tub, and think about the distance between the heater and the tub, since a long pipe wastes water while it warms.

A recirculation loop or a point-of-use heater shortens the wait. Those are design decisions for rough-in, not for the last day of the remodel.

Testing the tub before anything is covered

Plug the drain and fill the tub to the overflow. Let it sit for an hour, then release the water and watch every joint on the drain and overflow. Any drip means a gasket or a joint needs attention before the wall closes.

Run the supply lines under pressure as well. A slow gauge drop or a damp spot at a joint shows a leak long before a stain would. Take photos of the open wall with the pipes in place, so a future plumber knows where everything sits.

Finish with a hot and cold check at the spout and the shower arm. Reversed supplies are easy to fix with the wall open and expensive to fix after tile.

What shapes the cost of tub plumbing

A written scope keeps the price clear.

Tub type

Alcove, freestanding and soaking tubs differ in rough-in.

Drain relocation

Moving a drain is more work than a swap.

Valve and trim

Pressure-balancing valves cost more than basic ones.

Permit

Alteration of drain and vent piping needs one.

Access and finish

Panels and wall repair are part of the job.

Older West Hollywood buildings and tub drains

Many West Hollywood buildings were built before July 1, 1979, which puts them under rent stabilization and often means cast iron drains and galvanized or early copper supply. A tub replacement in an older building may meet a corroded drain stub or a valve that no longer matches modern trim. Plan for that surprise in the budget.

The West Hollywood Plumbing Code requires a permit for alteration of drain, waste and vent piping, and ordinary stoppage clearing is exempt. If you plan to move a tub drain, ask the city first.
